Ten accounts for Merge

Not companies hiring integration engineers. People who took ownership of the integration surface within the last year, at companies whose product does not work until it reaches a system the customer already runs.

Why a person and not a job posting. I began this week filtering accounts on open requisitions. It broke on the first company I tested: 737 live postings, not one of them the role I was looking for, and the same job duplicated eight times across cities. A posting count measures a company's ATS hygiene, not its need. A person who took the seat is a single fact and it does not duplicate.
What was checked, and how. Every contact was run against two independent data providers. Seven addresses sit on domains that reject unknown recipients; three sit on catch-all domains and are marked as corroborated rather than proven. Separately, I queried the mail records for all ten domains directly: ten of ten resolve to a live mail server — Microsoft 365, Google and Proofpoint. Seven of the ten LinkedIn profiles were confirmed to load; three returned LinkedIn's automated-traffic block rather than an answer, and are marked on the card as not machine-checked rather than described as verified.
Four candidates were cut during verification, not before it. One contact's address came back on two different domains from two providers. Two more could not be found at all by the second provider. A fourth was dropped when his record turned out to merge two different companies into a single entry, with eight concurrent current roles. What remains is flagged honestly: two of these companies are shrinking and one has a disputed employee count. Each says so on its own card.
