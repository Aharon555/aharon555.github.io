Ten accounts for Atera

One thesis, applied ten times: a lot of machines, in a lot of places, held together by a small team in the middle. Each of these ten put a named person into the IT operations seat within the last year, and every one of them is still deciding what that programme looks like.

Why these ten and not a hiring filter. I started this week filtering accounts on open requisitions. It failed on the first company I checked: 737 live postings, none of them the role I was looking for, and the same job duplicated eight times across cities. A posting count measures how a company posts, not what it needs. A person who took the seat is one fact and it does not duplicate. That is the whole selection rule here.
What I did not do. I did not check whether any of these ten already run a competing platform, and I have not spoken to any of them. Two are one month into the job and are marked as holds rather than targets. One address could not be resolved and one contact record is too thin to defend; both are flagged on their own cards rather than quietly dropped, because a list of ten with two honest warnings is worth more than a list of eight that looks perfect.
