Ten real estate funds that are open and still raising, from their own SEC filings.

A sponsor with a live offering has to file a Form D amendment every year it stays open. Nine of these ten filed one in August, which means the offering is still running and the investor count is current as of that filing. One is a brand-new Form D. The count on each card is the reporting burden, published by the buyer, with a date on it.

Where this came from

1,193 Form D filings mentioning real estate were pulled from SEC EDGAR full-text search for 1–27 August 2026. Each filing's raw XML was parsed for amount sold, investor count, date of first sale and whether it was an original filing or an annual amendment. Filings under $2M or with fewer than 10 investors were dropped, as were institutional managers who build their own systems.

The filing names the fund vehicle. The buyer is the sponsor firm behind it. Mapping one to the other is manual, and it is why this list is not something a database can produce.

What the amendment actually tells you

An original Form D is filed within 15 days of the first sale. After that, a sponsor must file an amendment every year the offering stays open. So a D/A in August is not a closed raise — it is the opposite. It is proof the fund is still taking money right now, and the investor count on it is current rather than historical. Roers has been raising since March 2022 and is still going. That is 53 months of one investor list growing under one team.

Only one account here filed a brand-new Form D: Cardone Equity Fund 29, first sale 30 July, 290 investors inside a month. It is flagged on its own card and it is the fastest-moving account on the list.
