Why developer experience and not security. The obvious list for a supply-chain product is heads of application security. That list is short, crowded and already being worked by everyone selling into the same category. The person who actually decides whether a dependency check survives is the one who owns the build, because they are measured on how fast engineers ship and they will remove anything that costs them that. Convincing them is harder and worth more.
Why a person and not a job posting. I began the week filtering accounts on open requisitions. It broke on the first company I tested: 737 live postings, none of them the role I was looking for, and the same job duplicated eight times across cities. A posting count measures a company's ATS hygiene, not its need. A person who took the seat is a single fact and it does not duplicate.
Where these are weak, said out loud. Five of the ten are product leaders rather than engineers, which means they shape the requirement and rarely sign for it. One seat date is contradicted between providers and is marked as unresolved rather than guessed. Two companies are barely growing. Each card says which problem it has.
