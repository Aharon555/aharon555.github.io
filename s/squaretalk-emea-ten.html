Read this first. This list is built on an assumption, because you have not told me yet. You asked what I do and I asked who you sell to. Rather than wait, I picked the assumption I could defend from your own site and built on it. If it is wrong, say which part and I will rebuild the list rather than argue for this one. Naming the assumption out loud is the whole method, so it would be strange to hide it here.
The assumption, and where it came from. Squaretalk sells a predictive dialer and local numbers in over a hundred and fifty destinations, described on your own site as a way to establish and maintain a local presence and support global expansion. A support desk does not need either of those. An outbound team calling into countries it does not sit in needs both. So I assumed your buyer is a company that has recently opened a new selling territory, and I looked for the moment that happens.
The signal, and why a filter will not find it. Each of these is a sales leader based in Europe whose company is headquartered in the United States, seated in the last four to twelve months. That comparison is between two fields that no filter compares: where the person is and where the company is. Apollo will sell you either one. It will not tell you they disagree, and the disagreement is the entire signal.
Where this list is weak, said out loud. Four of the ten are at the twelve month mark, which is the top of the window and the point where they may already have solved this. Three are at companies of a hundred and seventy or more, where a global vendor is probably already in place and this becomes a displacement rather than an install. One is an internal promotion rather than an arrival. Each card says which problem it has. Two companies were removed from the pool rather than left in to reach ten: Sabio, because it sells contact centre software and competes with you, and Rilla, because it sells to sales teams and would read as a conflict.
