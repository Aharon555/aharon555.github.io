Ten accounts for Cover

Ten Israeli HR leaders, each between four and ten months into a new seat, each at a company that added meaningful headcount while they were settling in. The seat date is the whole point. A person who inherited a benefits process is still deciding whether to keep it. A person who built it will defend it.

The pool was 78, not 4,000. Israeli HR leaders at 50 to 400 person companies, seated in the last twelve months, at companies with positive headcount growth. That is the entire population, and I would rather tell you the number than imply the list came from somewhere deeper. It also means this segment supports roughly two batches like this one per quarter before it repeats, which is worth knowing before anyone plans a year of outreach against it.
Why the seat date and not the headcount. Every benefits vendor filters on company size, so every HR leader in Israel hears from all of them at the same threshold. Growth alone does not create a decision either. What creates one is a person who arrived recently enough to still be auditing what they inherited. Ten to twelve months is roughly where that window closes, which is why the two people at ten months are ranked below the two at six and seven.
The openers are in Hebrew. Every one of these people works in Hebrew and your reps write in Hebrew. An English opener on this list would be a nice artifact that nobody sends. They are written to be sent as they are, or edited, not admired.
Where this list is weak, said out loud. Three companies are growing under 20%, which means nothing forces a decision there. Two people hold a C-level title and own budget, the rest recommend it. One email address is contradicted between the two providers and is marked unresolved rather than guessed. Cibus and pluxee were removed from the pool as direct competitors rather than left in to pad the count. Each card says which problem it has.

What was cut and why

The pool was 78 people. These are the decisions that took it to ten.

Cibus and pluxeeRemoved as a direct competitor in employee benefits rather than left in the pool to make the number look larger.
Companies with flat or negative growthExcluded. Without new headcount the existing process keeps working and there is no event to sell against.
Anyone under 4 months in seatExcluded. Too early to hold budget and usually still learning what they inherited.
Anyone over 12 months in seatExcluded. Past the window where the inherited process is still open to question.
Companies over 400 peopleExcluded. An enterprise HR system is usually already in place, which makes it a displacement sale on a much longer cycle.
Companies under 50 peopleExcluded. A spreadsheet genuinely still works at that size and the pain is not real yet.
Second contacts at the same companyExcluded. Ten accounts means ten companies. No company appears twice on this list.
