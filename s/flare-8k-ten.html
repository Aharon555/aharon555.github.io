Ten companies that told the SEC they had a cybersecurity incident.

Since 2023 a US public company has been legally required to report a material cybersecurity incident on Form 8-K under Item 1.05, within four business days of deciding it is material. These ten filed one this year. It is a disclosure the company had no choice about making, with a date on it, and it is not in any sales database.

Why this and not a breach feed

Breach news is downstream of a leak, a researcher or a ransom post. An Item 1.05 filing is upstream of all of that: it is the company itself, under legal obligation, telling a regulator that something material happened. It arrives on a fixed clock and it names the company without ambiguity.

The number on each card is how many times they filed. One filing means a contained event with a clean story. Four filings means the assessed scope kept moving for months. That count is the closest thing to a severity read that exists in public data.

Method

SEC EDGAR full-text search for Form 8-K filings containing Item 1.05, 1 January to 27 August 2026. That returned 42 filings from 19 distinct companies. Each company was then resolved to its security or IT owner, confirmed in seat with a start date. Nine of the nineteen are not here: either no reachable security owner exists in the record, or the company is too small to have one.
