A worked example · 27 August 2026 · re-read 29 August

Ten companies passed a hiring-salespeople filter. I read every posting behind them.

I said I would send you this. Here it is, re-run this morning so the dates are today's. Ten companies that all cleared the same filter — currently hiring Account Executive or SDR, posted since 1 July. Then I pulled all 297 raw postings and read them. Three were clean. Seven had something wrong.

Why this matters more than the hit rate

The interesting part is not that the filter is sometimes wrong. It is that it is wrong in seven different ways, and every one of them looks fine from the outside. A company that runs a job board. A company whose only US sales seat is an SDR. A posting last seen eight weeks ago. A req that has been open ten months. The same job counted three times. Postings with no country at all. And a company that is genuinely hiring, where the location field contradicts the job title on the same record.

None of that is visible until somebody opens the postings. The filter returns all ten identically.

The one worth reading twice

ADWEEK. Forty open postings, eight of them titled Advertising Account Executive. It looks like the best account on the list. Every single posting sits on jobs.adweek.com, because ADWEEK operates a job board — the roles belong to Cox Media, NYT Wirecutter, Lonely Planet and whoever else paid to list. ADWEEK is not hiring a single one of them. A data provider scraped a marketplace and attributed the inventory to the landlord.

What I would not claim

Ten is a small sample and I picked the companies from one filter on one day, so the seven-of-ten ratio is not a statistic. What it is, is ten specific companies you can go and check yourself. The postings are all public. Open ADWEEK's careers page and count how many of those jobs are theirs.

Three of the ten were completely clean — LlamaIndex, Ollama and Morning Brew. The filter told the truth about those. That is the part a vendor selling you a filter would also mention, and the part somebody selling you skepticism would leave out.

Method

Companies of 51–500 employees in the United States with an active posting for Account Executive, Enterprise Account Executive or Sales Development Representative dated on or after 1 July 2026. Staffing firms and consultancies removed by industry code. Then every raw posting for each company pulled and read: title, city, state, country, date posted, date last observed, and the URL it actually lives on.
